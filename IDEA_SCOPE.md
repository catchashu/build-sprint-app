IDEA LOCK · Build Sprint

The idea, in one line:
Topic Gibbon is an AI-native, pay-as-you-go topic research tool that turns a business or topic into a clear map of what its audience cares about, how those topics relate, and how they can translate into website structure, pages, FAQs and content, without subscriptions, giant spreadsheets or SEO-tool complexity.

Why me (at least 1 of 3: an audience that trusts me, years inside the workflow, data nobody else has):
I have deep experience inside this workflow as a marketer and consultant. I have repeatedly done keyword and topic research manually using tools like Semrush, Google, ChatGPT/Claude and spreadsheets, then turned large keyword sets into actual website structures, topic clusters and content decisions.

I have seen firsthand that the difficult part is usually not finding thousands of keywords. It is reducing them into the relatively small number of topics that actually matter and deciding what to do with them.

I also have access to marketers, founders, consultants, GrowthX members and past students who face this problem.

GOAL

The one goal they hire it for (money, time, status or life):
Time.

Help someone go from “I need to understand what my customers care about and search for” to “I understand the topic landscape and know how my website/content should address it” much faster.

Delta 4 (the steps today → the steps with my product):

Today:
1. Decide seed topics.
2. Search Google and inspect suggestions, related searches and People Also Ask.
3. Use Semrush/Ahrefs/Ubersuggest/Keyword Planner or similar tools.
4. Repeat searches to expand the topic and keyword universe.
5. Export multiple lists.
6. Clean and deduplicate them in spreadsheets.
7. Cluster keywords manually or with another tool.
8. Interpret intent, customer stage and relationships between topics.
9. Decide which topics deserve pages, sections, FAQs or supporting content.
10. Turn everything into a usable website/topic architecture.

With Topic Gibbon:
1. Tell Gibbon about the business/topic and what you are trying to achieve.
2. Answer a few adaptive questions so Gibbon understands the business and audience.
3. Gibbon researches the topic using AI plus real search/SERP evidence, expands it, clusters it and interprets the relationships.
4. Receive a clear topic map and suggested website/content structure, with the supporting search evidence available when needed.

The sin it rides (optional):
Sloth.

It removes a tedious, multi-tool research and spreadsheet workflow and makes a complicated job dramatically easier.

USER

The trigger (when the pain hits):
When someone is starting or restructuring a website, entering a new category, planning content, researching a client/business, launching or adding a service, working on a local business, or is simply told:

“Figure out what our customers care about, what they search for and what our website should cover.”

Today's path, step by step (including not solving it at all):
They usually begin with Google or ChatGPT, generate some seed keywords, inspect autocomplete, related searches and People Also Ask, use free keyword tools or temporarily subscribe to a tool like Semrush, export large lists, move them into spreadsheets, clean and cluster them, and then manually work out what the important topics actually are.

Some users never get past the keyword-list stage. Others stop because the workflow becomes too complicated, expensive or time-consuming.

Who they trust on this decision:
Experienced marketers and search/SEO practitioners, their agency or consultant, marketing peers, tools such as Semrush/Ahrefs, Google itself and the underlying search/SERP evidence.

Would they pay? (what exists today that people pay for):
Yes.

People already pay for Semrush, Ahrefs, Ubersuggest, Keyword Insights, Keyword Cupid, topical-map tools, SEO consultants and agencies.

There is also a common behaviour among founders and smaller marketers of subscribing to a professional tool for a short period, extracting the research they need and then cancelling it.

Topic Gibbon is designed for someone who needs the research outcome without committing to another recurring SaaS subscription.

PRODUCT

Onboarding (how a first-time user feels the value fastest):
A minimal conversational interface with the Gibbon character.

The user simply describes the business/topic they want to understand.

Gibbon asks a small number of adaptive questions about the business, customer and objective, then starts revealing the important branches of the topic.

There are no complex dashboards, long forms or SEO configuration screens before the user experiences value.

The core loop (user stories, written by me):
1. I tell Gibbon what business/topic I want to research and what I am trying to achieve.
2. I answer a few questions so Gibbon understands my business, customer and context.
3. Gibbon expands the topic using AI plus search/SERP evidence from APIs such as SerpAPI/search-data providers.
4. Gibbon groups the research into meaningful topic clusters rather than overwhelming me with thousands of keywords.
5. I see the topic as a simple, understandable map.
6. I can understand which topics represent high-intent/commercial needs, consideration-stage questions and broader discovery/education needs.
7. For a services/business website, I can see which topics may deserve core service/category pages, supporting sections, FAQs or informational content.
8. I can open a topic branch to inspect the important underlying searches, questions and supporting signals.
9. I leave with a clear understanding of what my audience cares about and how my website/content architecture should broadly reflect it.

Coming back (optional):
The user comes back whenever they need to research another category, service, location, client or business.

The commercial model can be pay-as-you-go or per research/project rather than requiring a recurring subscription.

The AI-first part (onboarding, engagement or the core loop):
Both onboarding and the core loop are AI-first.

AI understands the user's business context, decides which follow-up questions matter, expands the research, interprets SERP/search evidence, semantically groups related topics, understands intent and customer stage, and explains the resulting structure in plain language.

Without AI, Topic Gibbon becomes another keyword database or clustering tool.

A core product philosophy is that keywords and search volumes are inputs, not the final product. Gibbon is trying to understand the topic and customer landscape.

MARKET

Tailwinds (where funding is going, what Google Trends shows, timing):
The main tailwind is not renewed interest in traditional keyword research.

It is the changing way people discover information through Google, AI search, answer engines and LLM-based interfaces.

Traditional keyword SEO fundamentals still matter, but simply choosing the highest-volume keyword is no longer enough. Businesses increasingly need to understand the full set of topics, questions, entities and customer needs surrounding their category.

For AEO/GEO and answer-engine visibility, businesses also need useful, specific content across the questions and stages their customers care about so that search and answer systems have relevant information to retrieve and cite.

At the same time, AI and accessible SERP/search APIs make it much cheaper to research, interpret and organise this information than it was when traditional SEO platforms were built.

Topic Gibbon is built around this shift from “give me more keywords” to “help me understand and cover my topic properly.”

Competitors (and the flows I liked, with screenshots and why):
Direct and adjacent competitors include Semrush, Ahrefs, Ubersuggest, Keyword Insights, Keyword Cupid, Topical Map AI and other keyword/topic-clustering products.

I am already familiar with the standard workflow across these tools: enter seed keywords, expand the keyword set, filter, inspect metrics, export, cluster and interpret the results.

What I like:
- Semrush/Ahrefs: depth of search and keyword data.
- Keyword/topic clustering tools: reducing large keyword sets into groups.
- Topic-map/mind-map interfaces: showing relationships visually.
- AI-native products: conversational onboarding rather than long forms.

What I want to do differently:
Topic Gibbon should not feel like another professional SEO dashboard.

It should make interpretation the default experience, prioritise topics and customer questions over giant keyword lists, use real SERP/search evidence underneath the AI layer, remove the need for a recurring subscription, and create a beautiful, memorable guided experience through the Gibbon character.
