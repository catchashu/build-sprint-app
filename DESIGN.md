

\#\# 1\. The Feeling, In Labels

\*\*Product direction:\*\* A clear, trustworthy research workspace set inside a living green forest canopy, accompanied by a believable, expressive Gibbon.

The visual system uses five core treatments.

\#\#\# Living Forest Canopy

Natural green foliage, layered environmental depth, soft filtered light & a believable forest atmosphere. The environment should feel alive without making the working interface visually noisy.

Avoid flat jungle illustrations, neon greenery or decorative effects that compete with the task.

\#\#\# Believable Gibbon Warmth

A mature, sturdy, moderately fluffy Gibbon with a light cream/golden coat, pale facial border, dark facial skin, naturalistic anatomy, long arms & recognisable grasping hands and feet.

The character feels soft and dimensional, but never like a cartoon, toy or baby Gibbon. Preserve its approved facial construction, body volume & identity across all states.

\#\#\# Frosted Working Surface

A pale, warm-off-white translucent surface over the forest, with subtle foliage visible behind it. Text and controls remain fully readable.

The surface provides a calm contrast to the natural environment. Use the exact frost, blur, contrast & fallback specifications in Section 3\.

\#\#\# Quiet Interface, Expressive Character

The Gibbon brings warmth & communicates progress. The interface remains focused on the user's task.

Movement can be lively during appropriate transitions or processing states, but becomes restrained when the user is reading, entering information or making decisions.

\#\#\# Clear, Evidence-Led Workspace

Strong typography, one visually dominant primary action per decision state, restrained use of green, predictable controls & progressive disclosure of complexity.

The experience should feel approachable without hiding important decisions or research evidence.

\*\*Visual consistency:\*\* Use the approved colours, Inter typography, spacing scale, component shapes & scene composition defined in Section 3\. Section 4 governs how the environment and character behave in each state. Section 5 governs user-facing words.

\*\*Avoid:\*\* Unapproved cartoon styling, permanently smiling expressions, excessive character motion, colourful promotional UI, decorative gradients, inconsistent character proportions, competing primary actions or vague AI-generated marketing copy.

## 3\. Type and colour

**Font:** Inter throughout. Use 400 for regular text, 600 for emphasis & primary controls, and 700 for the main headline. No second typeface.

**Sizes:** Display 28–48px responsive for the main headline or one primary result · heading 20px · body 16px · small 14px. Use relative units & only these four named sizes; do not shrink body, input or primary-button text on phones.

**Text:** Fully opaque dark ink \#1D2723; secondary text \#55605A. Place text on a centred, pale frosted panel over the full-screen green forest/canopy. Panel tint: \#F7F8F5 at 90% opacity with 16px backdrop blur. Frost the surface, not the letters. Keep inputs opaque white; use solid \#F7F8F5 as the panel fallback, not the page background.

**Accent:** Solid deep green \#21634A with white text, only on the current screen/state’s main action, as defined in Section 4\. Secondary actions stay neutral. Hover/pressed: \#194D39. Keep the main-action button opaque on the pale panel. The green scenery is separate from the interface-accent rule.

**Errors:** Red \#B42318, paired with an explanatory message & relevant indicator. Never communicate an error through colour alone.

### Essential implementation rules

**Gibbon & composition:** **Reserve the upper one-third of the initial landing-screen height for the Gibbon**, not one-third of the scrollable page. The frosted panel starts below it. Preserve this space even before artwork loads. Centre the panel, heading & narrower form on the same horizontal axis, with canopy visible in the gutters. Later proportions follow Section 4\. Allow vertical scrolling rather than reclaiming the Gibbon space.

**Type behaviour:** Display line height 1.12 & letter spacing −0.03em; heading 1.30 & −0.01em; body/small 1.50 & normal spacing. Copy-specific headline breaks are in Section 5\. Spacing, tap-size & check rules are in Section 6\.

**Information controls:** What? · Why? · How? · For me? are quiet 14px text-and-information-icon triggers, not outlined navigation buttons. Use one centred row when it fits, or a balanced two-by-two arrangement on narrow phones. Follow Section 4 for the shared explainer’s opening, closing & audience tabs, and Section 5 for its words. Do not display expanded answers permanently below the form.

**Detailed reference:** topic-gibbon-design-section-3-v1-revised in the project folder. Retain this as the implementation reference; this section is the simplified design.md specification.

### \#\#\# Topic Gibbon Product Canvas

For: Complete the Topic Gibbon job inside one continuous forest/canopy environment after Start.

This is one actual screen, not a sequence of conventional pages. The Product Canvas changes state while the Gibbon communicates what the product is doing.

Canvas layout rule: use a mobile-first two-zone system. The upper Gibbon Stage is the expressive character space and normally gets the majority of the viewport after Start. The lower Interaction Tray is a frosted/high-contrast work surface anchored to the bottom. At rest, the tray is roughly the lower third of the screen; it may expand toward about half the viewport for card-heavy, correction, payment, or review states. Prefer internal tray scrolling before allowing it to swallow the full Gibbon Stage. Questions, inputs, cards, corrections, payment, and decision controls live in the Interaction Tray; Gibbon motion lives primarily in the Stage.

Keyboard rule: when a text field receives focus on mobile, the Interaction Tray rises above the system keyboard and the Gibbon Stage compresses to a small presence strip. If the device leaves too little usable space, the Gibbon may temporarily disappear while the keyboard is open rather than compromising input legibility. The full Stage returns immediately when the keyboard closes. Desktop preserves the same separation of character space and work surface, but does not need to follow the mobile percentages literally.

State rule: during Arrival, keep the Interaction Tray minimal until the Gibbon settles; during active processing, the tray may collapse to a compact status strip so the Gibbon has room to perform; during user-decision states, the tray expands only as much as the task requires.

\#\# 6\. Principles

These rules apply across Topic Gibbon unless an explicitly documented state-specific exception exists.

1\. \*\*One consistent Gibbon.\*\* Use the approved adult Gibbon identity for every expression, pose, screen & animation. Preserve its coat, face, body volume, proportions, hands and feet. Do not independently regenerate the character for each frame or state.

2\. \*\*One continuous Product Canvas.\*\* After Start, the experience remains within the established forest/canopy canvas. Screen states change without unnecessary conventional page navigation.

3\. \*\*One clear primary action per decision state.\*\* Secondary actions remain visually subordinate. Navigation, information triggers & correction controls must not compete with the main next step.

4\. \*\*Motion serves the task.\*\* The Gibbon communicates activity, progress & transitions through the approved Motion Bible. It remains quiet when the user needs to read, type, review evidence or decide. Movement must not delay or obstruct the product task.

5\. \*\*User input takes priority over character visibility.\*\* The Interaction Tray may expand for complex decisions. When the mobile keyboard opens, the Gibbon Stage compresses or temporarily disappears if necessary. Never sacrifice readable inputs or controls to preserve character space.

6\. \*\*Consequential AI interpretations can be corrected.\*\* Provide the state-appropriate correction mechanism. Preserve accepted context, ask only what is necessary and avoid restarting unaffected work where practical.

7\. \*\*Show decisions before evidence depth.\*\* Present understandable first-level results. Reveal supporting rationale, demand areas, queries & provenance progressively. Do not overwhelm the initial view with raw research.

8\. \*\*Reuse the approved design system.\*\* Inter throughout. Use the Display, Heading, Body & Small tokens from Section 3\. Use the spacing scale of 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 & 64px and one 10px corner radius. No unapproved colours, sizes, radii or component patterns.

9\. \*\*Use weight & contrast before size.\*\* Keep normal body, field & button text readable. Allow reflow at enlarged text sizes. Do not rewrite approved copy, clip text or reduce legibility merely to match a specimen screenshot.

10\. \*\*Protect accessibility & usability.\*\* Primary actions have a minimum 48px height. Information & close controls have minimum 44 × 44px tap areas. Preserve visible keyboard focus, meaningful non-colour status cues and readable contrast against actual rendered backgrounds.

11\. \*\*Preserve user progress during recoverable failures.\*\* Keep confirmed inputs, corrections, unlock status & completed research wherever possible. Retry the affected step rather than silently restarting the run. Character animation failure must not block product completion.

12\. \*\*Verify the actual responsive product.\*\* Review at 320px, 390px & 1280px and with enlarged text. Check stage allocation, scrolling, headline wrapping, panel centring, keyboard behaviour, focus visibility, character readability & state-specific controls.

13\. \*\*Approved references outrank generic AI design choices.\*\* If a requested change conflicts with the Character Bible, Motion Bible, approved components, screen definitions or exact copy, ask before changing the established design.

14\. \*\*Keep design principles separate from one-off fixes.\*\* Record permanent cross-screen rules here. Keep individual screen treatments in Section 4, copy in Section 5 & technical implementation details in their appropriate specifications.
