How To Use This Document  
The Sheet is the evidence database. Every reference video is logged with terrain, camera angle, timing, body mechanics, caveats and V1 implications. This Motion Bible is the synthesis layer: once patterns repeat across references, they become canonical instructions for the Topic Gibbon.  
Rule: do not promote a motion detail to canonical unless it is either supported by multiple references or deliberately chosen as a stylized design decision. When evidence is thin, label the spec provisional.

# Motion Research Protocol

# 1\. The user supplies the reference URL, timestamp range and motion family. Treat those as the exact scope.

# 2\. Always run the specified footage through the vidIQ visual-watch connector first. Do not substitute web search, transcript reading or generic animal knowledge for the requested motion analysis.

# 3\. When captions, subtitles, speech or overlays are irrelevant, explicitly tell vidIQ to ignore them and analyze visible motion only.

# 4\. Do not search for additional videos unless the user explicitly asks. Video discovery / outlier search is off by default.

# 5\. Old source videos may be reused with new timestamps for a different motion family. Create a new reference ID for the new motion analysis.

# 6\. Separate direct observation from Topic Gibbon design interpretation. Mark blur, occlusion, angle limits and uncertain anatomy explicitly.

# 7\. After every successful motion analysis, update all four layers: Reference Library → Motion Index → Canonical Motion Specs → Motion Bible.

# 8\. Canonical promotion rule: one useful reference \= provisional; corroborating independent references \= stronger provisional; promote to V1-ready when the key mechanics, variation and/or viewing angles are adequately covered.

# 9\. Do not turn individual quirks into biological rules. Hand preference, exact asymmetry and context-specific actions stay variable unless multiple references support them.

# 10\. V1 motions should be modular and interruptible. Animation must serve product state and hand off cleanly at natural breakpoints when AI processing completes.

# V1 Motion Scope

V1 uses one Topic Gibbon character. Motion should make the product feel alive and delightful while still serving a single-screen AI flow. The character should never become visual noise or make the interface harder to use, especially on mobile.

# 0\. Arrival / Canopy Emergence

## Status

# V1-ready. REF-ARRIVAL-001 through REF-ARRIVAL-003 now establish the hybrid entrance across frontal, front-three-quarter and full side/profile views, including free/direct descent, flexible-branch-assisted descent, and a no-hand-brace landing.

## Current Motion Rule

# The Topic Gibbon's default entrance begins with a brief environmental cue before the character is fully visible. Foliage or a branch moves first, then the gibbon emerges from the canopy edge in a controlled downward swing / drop. The body stays compact with flexed legs; in a direct-release descent the legs may extend only near touchdown, while a supported variant may keep one hand attached until the feet reach the ground. The landing is feet-first and absorbed through deep hip and knee flexion. A brief hand / knuckle stabilization can occur but is not required: side-view evidence shows the lower body can absorb the landing alone while the arms remain high for balance. Crucially, the landing never becomes a held pose; compression rebounds directly into the first bipedal strides. The result is one continuous canopy-to-ground arrival ending in a clear walk toward or across the user view.

The gibbon then drops / swings downward into frontal open view with arms extended and legs tucked. It lands feet-first, compresses deeply through the hips and knees, briefly brushes the ground with one hand for balance, and immediately rebounds into a frontal bipedal walk / run toward the camera. There is virtually no static pause between landing and locomotion. Captions and on-screen text were explicitly ignored during analysis.  
REF-ARRIVAL-002: 00:00–00:04. A front / front-three-quarter entrance using a flexible branch. The canopy and branch visibly bow and rustle before the gibbon fully emerges. The feet reach the asphalt while the hand still carries some branch load; the hand then releases and the branch recoils upward. The hips and knees compress deeply on touchdown, a light knuckle/hand stabilization may appear, and the body immediately transitions into high-arm bipedal walking with no sustained pause. ## Core Motion Rules

Across speeds and viewing angles, the Topic Gibbon moves bipedally with the hips and knees persistently flexed rather than using a straight-kneed human gait. Side-view evidence confirms a true crouched sagittal posture with a mild-to-moderate forward torso pitch and rhythmic vertical dipping through the pelvis and body. Front, rear-three-quarter and direct-rear views confirm pronounced lateral trunk sway plus alternating pelvic elevation/drop and rotation synchronized with the stride. Wrists and hands remain loose rather than rigid, and the head stays comparatively stable relative to the moving torso and pelvis. Arm carriage is variable and balance/context-dependent rather than controlled by speed alone. Faster movement often raises the arms, but relaxed walking is supported with both low/outward arms (REF-WALK-004) and high-abducted arms (REF-WALK-008). The invariant is independent balance use of the long arms, not one fixed arm height.

## Relaxed Walk Variation

REF-WALK-004 and REF-WALK-008 together define the relaxed-walk range. Both show continuous ground support with no flight phase, deliberate cadence, persistent hip/knee flexion, smooth lateral sway, loose hands and a stable head. REF-WALK-004 shows a low/outward-arm calm variant, while REF-WALK-008 shows the same relaxed lower-body mechanics from rear/rear-three-quarter with high-abducted arms. Therefore high arms are not exclusive to faster locomotion. For V1, preserve the calm gait invariants and allow arm carriage to vary with balance/context.

## Must Preserve

• Long arms held clear of the ground and used as balancing levers; height may vary with balance/context, not speed alone  
• Bent-knee, bent-hip locomotion  
• Obvious lateral weight shift / body roll, with subtle pelvic elevation/drop and rotation visible from behind  
• Loose wrists and relaxed fingers  
• Asymmetrical, independent arm movement  
• Head more stable than the torso and pelvis, including in side profile  
• A rhythm that reads immediately as non-human rather than a person with long arms

## Can Vary

• Exact arm height; both low/outward and high-abducted carriage are supported at relaxed pace  
• Which arm is higher at a given moment  
• Speed and stride length  
• Amount of forward torso lean, which may increase with acceleration  
• Degree of bounce  
• Exact foot angle and contact pattern, depending on terrain and speed

## Avoid

• Straight-kneed human running  
• Symmetrical human-style arm pumping  
• Rigid wrists or posed hands  
• Minimal lateral sway  
• Copying the stair reference’s forward lean and bounding into every terrain  
• Over-smoothing the movement until the distinctive awkwardness disappears

## V1 Simplification

Do not try to simulate every biomechanical detail. Exaggerate a small set of unmistakable cues: flexed knees, side-to-side sway, subtle pelvic roll, loose wrists, a comparatively steady head and independently balancing long arms. Build at least two readable pace states: a relaxed walk with slower cadence and no flight phase, and faster ground locomotion with quicker cadence and stronger forward pitch. Arm height should not be hard-coded to speed: the relaxed walk may use low/outward or high-abducted carriage. These cues should remain legible from front, side and rear views. The goal is recognisable gibbon movement with a stylized animation system, not wildlife-documentary realism.

# 2\. Stop / Sit / Settle

## Status

V1-ready. Three references now cover stair/wet-deck, relaxed flat-ground front, and flat-ground slight side / three-quarter. The transition is stable enough to specify for V1.

## Current Motion Rule

The Topic Gibbon should visibly decelerate before sitting. Final strides shorten, forward travel reduces, and the centre of mass drops mostly vertically through deep ankle, knee and hip flexion. The pelvis settles into a low, wide seated / semi-crouched base. The arms transition from locomotion balance into descent balance and then directly toward the next interaction. Ground support is optional: a faster or less stable stop may use one brief hand brace, while the default flat-ground sit can remain entirely lower-body driven with both hands free. Small pelvic, haunch and foot adjustments complete the settle. The head stays comparatively steady toward the interaction target, and a distinct stable beat marks entry into the ready/input state.

## Settled / Input-Ready Pose

REF-SIT-001 (00:05–00:08 of the stair clip) gives us the first dedicated settle reference. After the braking steps, the Topic Gibbon lowers into a low, wide semi-crouched seat with knees flared and feet planted close under the body. The left hand briefly braces on the surface while the right arm remains available for the upcoming food/context interaction. Small shifts through the pelvis and feet continue after contact. By roughly 00:07–00:08 the gross body motion has stopped: the torso is visually stable, the head remains forward, and the character reads as ready for the next interaction. The exact amount of pelvic contact is uncertain because of the camera angle and dark fur, so the design should preserve the function of the pose rather than copy that detail literally.

## Flat-Ground Confirmation

REF-SIT-002 (00:05–00:07 of the relaxed-walk clip) gives us the independent flat-ground comparison we needed. The gibbon slows over the final two steps, widens the feet, and lowers the centre of mass almost vertically through deep ankle, knee and hip flexion. Unlike the stair reference, neither hand touches the ground: the descent is entirely lower-body driven, with the arms moving forward/outward for balance. The pelvis makes direct contact with the road, then shifts slightly across the haunches before becoming visually stable by about 00:07. This confirms that the support-hand touch in REF-SIT-001 is optional and context-dependent rather than a required part of the sit.

## Side / Three-Quarter Confirmation

REF-SIT-003 (00:46–00:48) closes the remaining V1 angle gap. From the slight side / three-quarter view, the gibbon shortens the final steps, lowers the centre of mass almost straight down through deep ankle, knee and hip flexion, and settles directly onto the asphalt. Neither hand touches the ground. Instead, the arms transition immediately from locomotion balance into the forward/downward reach toward the offered food. Minor foot and torso adjustments complete the settle, and full visual stability is reached by about 00:48. This confirms that the default flat-ground Topic Gibbon sit can move directly from balance into interaction without inserting a support-hand phase.

## Must Preserve

• A visible braking beat rather than an instant pose change  
• Final strides shortening before the vertical drop  
• A clear drop in body height  
• Arms changing role from locomotion balance to descent / settling balance  
• Weight visibly transferring into the seated posture  
• Hand support is optional and context-dependent, not mandatory  
• Small micro-adjustments after contact  
• A visually stable ready beat before the next interaction

# 3\. Receive Food / Receive Context

## Status

# V1-ready. Four references now show the same core receiving mechanics across settled, pre-settled and repeated grabs. The latest peanut clip adds three successful pickups in one video and confirms a reusable reach → grip → retract → reset loop.

## Current Motion Rule

# The Topic Gibbon receives an offering with one long-arm reach from a controlled low crouched / seated state. Full static settling is not required: the reach may begin while the pelvis is still completing its descent, or it may begin from a fully seated base. The reaching shoulder moves forward and the elbow extends smoothly along a low, direct path. The wrist stays relaxed and slightly pronated. Before contact, the long fingers pre-curve into a hook-like shape, then sweep and curl inward to wrap the object into the palm/base rather than using a fine human fingertip pinch. Once secured, the elbow flexes quickly and retracts the object toward the body or mouth with minimal extra torso movement. For repeated grabs, the reaching arm simply resets downward toward the offering and repeats the same loop. The opposite hand may brace or remain free depending on stability needs.

# 4\. Eat / Inspect / Manipulate / Discard

## Status

# V1-ready. REF-EAT-001, REF-EAT-002 and REF-EAT-003 now cover large-fruit multi-item feeding, oral/manual discard, and a close alternate-food small-morsel sequence. The core modular eating mechanics are stable enough to specify for V1.

## Current Motion Rule

# The Topic Gibbon processes food from a stable seated base using modular, interruptible sub-actions. It may secure one item or several. For larger or multiple fruits, one hand can retain a reserve while the other feeds/manipulates. For a small morsel, one active hand can repeatedly inspect, lift, bite or shear, lower and reorient the item between bites. The hand-to-mouth path is short and direct; the head may dip slightly to meet it. Chewing is rhythmic, while gaze quickly decouples from the food to scan left, right and upward. Food can be rotated or digit-rolled before another bite. Unwanted pits, skin or remnants may be casually dropped/spat or cleared manually, but discard is optional when the item can be consumed directly. Small independent upper-limb actions can coexist with chewing. At natural breakpoints, the loop can reset to another bite/item or hand off into waiting, result-ready, or locomotion.

Around the first discard, small debris is dropped/spat from the mouth and residual material is cleared with the fingers. The second fruit is then picked up, bitten, rotated/reoriented in the hand, bitten again, chewed, and small remnants are discarded before the hand lowers into reset.

# The Topic Gibbon can occupy a low-energy processing / waiting state in a compact seated posture with deeply flexed hips and knees, a relaxed rounded spine, and long arms either gathered around the legs or draped passively along the lap / knees / substrate. 5C. Swing / Brachiation \- Core Loop, Hang, Direction Change & Exit

## Status

## Current Motion Rule

Steady-state brachiation is a continuous alternating single-arm pendulum. One arm supports and elongates under load through the downswing while the free arm reaches toward the next hold; near the upswing the new hand catches and the previous hand releases with little prolonged two-hand dwell. Legs stay flexed overall with modest extension / re-tuck modulation and the torso rotates toward the reach. The route does not need to remain linear: at corners or orthogonal supports, a loaded pivot arm plus torso yaw and redirected free-arm reach can carry momentum through 90° or 180° changes. Natural canopies can produce larger drop arcs and irregular branch-to-branch trajectories. Rare energetic variations may include pivoting / looping around one support or rebound transfers on compliant branches, but these are optional flourishes rather than baseline rules. Longer processing may decay into a quiet hang and rebuild into motion through core / leg tuck and renewed shoulder engagement. Exits may resolve into perch, supported landing, or direct steep descent to ground.

# Design Principle

The Topic Gibbon’s movement should communicate product state and create delight. It should never behave like a decorative loop pasted behind the interface.
