# **Consolidated Character-Building Principles | Working Summary**

The following points summarise the strongest recurring directions that have emerged across all six batches. These remain working character-building principles until a first controlled artwork pass is reviewed and approved.

C-12. The character should read as a believable, dimensional, furry gibbon with plush-like warmth, not as a flat cartoon and not as a visibly stitched toy.

C-13. Neutral facial expression should be calm and readable. The character should not require a smile or exaggerated eyebrows to appear alive.

C-14. Neutral-mouth construction and action-mouth construction should remain separate. Closed-mouth face references and open-mouth eating or calling references serve different jobs.

C-15. The body must preserve long arms, long grasping hands, and grasping feet. Do not shorten or soften them into generic cute mascot limbs.

C-16. Hanging and suspension are central to the silhouette language. The character should still look like itself when seated, perched, walking, reaching or hanging.

C-17. Hands and feet need believable grip logic. Fingers and toes should wrap or rest in ways consistent with real gibbon anatomy.

C-18. Keep palms and soles visually distinct from fur so contact points remain readable.

C-19. Use fur direction, layered strands and gravity-sensitive fullness. The coat should behave differently between seated and suspended poses.

C-20. Plush-like warmth should come from softness of fur, dimensional rendering and tactile presence, not from shrinking anatomy or making the body uniformly round.

C-21. Check profile coherence carefully: muzzle depth, face border shape, forehead and head-to-neck transition must stay consistent across frontal and side-oriented views.

C-22. Use structural diagrams and collages as anatomy support only, not as style references.

C-23. Watermarked or annotated references remain study aids only and are not approved for direct reproduction.

# **First Controlled Artwork Brief | Working v0.1**

This brief is the first character-generation task after the appearance-reference phase. The purpose is to establish the visual identity of the Topic Gibbon character, not to design the product interface, not to test the full state flow, and not to finalise motion. The result should be a small, controlled comparison that makes it easy to choose the preferred appearance direction.

## **Objective**

Generate the Topic Gibbon as a designed character based on the approved appearance-reference direction: believable, dimensional, furry, naturalistic, plush-like in warmth, and clearly recognisable as a gibbon. The first pass should answer one question only: “Is this our Gibbon?”

## **What This Pass Is Testing**

• Face construction and neutral expression

• Overall body proportions and silhouette

• Fur treatment, softness and dimensionality

• Readability of hands, feet and contact points

• Whether the character feels like a designed character rather than a wildlife photograph, flat cartoon or generic mascot

## **What This Pass Is Not Testing**

• The product UI or canopy screen layout

• The full motion system or screen-state mapping

• Open-mouth calling or eating animation

• Different costumes, props, accessories or expressions

• A whole character sheet with many poses

## **Best Reference Groups To Use**

Neutral face and calm seated character: REF-CHAR-006, REF-CHAR-020, REF-CHAR-025, REF-CHAR-029.

Body proportions and side or three-quarter body understanding: REF-CHAR-002, REF-CHAR-010, REF-CHAR-013, REF-CHAR-017, REF-CHAR-018, REF-CHAR-019.

Suspension and long-arm silhouette, for anatomical truth even if not used in the first pose: REF-CHAR-008, REF-CHAR-009, REF-CHAR-014, REF-CHAR-015.

Hands, feet and grip structure: REF-CHAR-021, REF-CHAR-022, REF-CHAR-023, REF-CHAR-024, REF-CHAR-027, REF-CHAR-028.

## **Controlled Output Request**

Request three variants only. Keep the pose, framing and overall setup substantially the same across all three. Vary only the rendering balance and softness slightly, so comparison stays clean.

Variant A: More naturalistic / wildlife-grounded

Variant B: Balanced character version, the most likely target

Variant C: Slightly softer and more plush-like, without becoming toy-like

## **Pose For The First Pass**

Use a calm seated or lightly perched three-quarter pose. The character should be full-body and clearly visible, not tightly bunched up. A simple perch, branch or low log is acceptable if it helps the body read well. The face should be in a calm, closed-mouth neutral expression. Hands and feet should be visible enough to judge them, but they do not need to be presented as separate close-ups.

## **Visual Direction**

• Believable, dimensional, furry gibbon character

• Warm cream-to-buff coat territory, with darker facial skin and clear fur-to-skin boundaries

• Long arms, long grasping hands, and grasping feet preserved

• Soft, tactile fur with visible strand breakup and layered fullness

• Naturalistic face, not babyfied and not permanently smiling

• Designed character treatment rather than a literal wildlife photograph

• A clean, uncluttered background so the character is easy to judge

## **Do Not Do**

• Do not make it a flat cartoon, sticker or simplified mascot

• Do not make it look like a stitched plush toy, teddy bear or generic monkey

• Do not shorten the limbs, enlarge the head excessively or chubbify the hands and feet

• Do not use a broad smile, human-like eyebrows or exaggerated cute expression

• Do not hide the hands and feet inside fur masses

• Do not add clothing, accessories, labels or props beyond a minimal perch if needed

• Do not build the whole forest interface or translucent UI panel in this pass

## **Evaluation Questions**

• Does this look like a specific gibbon character, not just any animal image?

• Does the face feel calm, readable and natural?

• Do the hands and feet still feel long, distinct and believable?

• Is the fur soft and plush-like without turning the body into a toy?

• Does the character feel suitable for later motion and product use?

• Across the three variants, which balance of realism and softness feels most right?

## **Recommended Prompt Skeleton For Generation**

Generate three controlled character-concept variants of the same Topic Gibbon. The character is a believable, dimensional, furry gibbon with plush-like warmth, based on the supplied references. Keep all three variants in substantially the same full-body seated or lightly perched three-quarter pose, with a calm closed-mouth neutral expression and visible hands and feet. Preserve long arms, long grasping hands, grasping feet, a naturalistic face, and warm cream-to-buff fur with darker facial skin. Use a clean, uncluttered background. Variant A should lean more naturalistic, Variant B should be the balanced target character, and Variant C should be slightly softer and more plush-like, but none should become toy-like, flat-cartoonish or overly cute. Do not add UI, clothing, accessories or extra props.

## **Follow-Up After First Review**

After choosing the preferred appearance variant, the next step should be a very small consistency set: one front or near-front calm view, one side or three-quarter profile check, and one hanging or suspended pose. Only after that should the character proceed into motion or product-context integration.
