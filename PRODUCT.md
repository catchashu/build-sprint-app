# \#\# 1\. The job

When the designer, client or stakeholder asks me to finalise the page list for a new website around an already-established offering, I want to understand what people are looking for and what deserves to be covered first, so I can submit a prioritised website page/content plan that gets approved and moves into production.  
Other moments it happens: When an established offering is getting a website revamp or a market-specific website plan and the page/content structure has to be rethought.

Who, by situation (not age, city or title): Someone who personally has to turn market/search evidence into a defensible website page/content plan before design and content production can begin.  
Today they hire: Their own experience and judgement, manual Google/SERP research, competitor websites, SEO tools such as Semrush, PAA/related searches, SME input, spreadsheets, agencies/consultants, or a combination of these.

(More than one person in the product? Write a job for each, and mark the one you build for this weekend.)

(Advanced, optional) What needs doing: Decide which things people are looking for deserve website coverage and put the resulting pages/content into a priority order.  
(Advanced, optional) How they want to feel: Confident enough to commit to the recommendation, knowing what the evidence supports and where uncertainty remains.  
(Advanced, optional) How they want to look to others: Like a rigorous, experienced marketer/strategist who understands the category and can justify the choices with evidence.

(B2B only) The bench: end user \[the hands-on marketing practitioner doing the research and preparing the prioritised page/content plan\], decision maker \[the client, founder or marketing leader approving the website direction\], who pays \[the business/client organisation, or occasionally the practitioner themselves\]  
The one we serve first: The hands-on practitioner who has to produce the prioritised website page/content plan and get it approved.

# \#\# 2\. The switch

What they'd fire: Manual keyword/topic research using Google SERPs, PAA, related searches, Keyword Planner or Semrush when available, spreadsheets, copy-pasting, clustering and their own judgement.

The forces that matter (keep one to four, delete the rest):  
Push, outside them (what's going wrong in their life right now): The website plan is waiting on research, and manual demand research becomes the bottleneck before design/content production can move.  
Pull, inside them (what they've always wanted): A prioritised website page/content plan they can defend, with the real searches behind the recommendation.  
Anxiety (the risk of trying you: what they tried before, or a decision that's hard to undo): Is this showing me real search demand, or is the AI just giving me plausible-looking topics it made up?  
Habit (the way they already do it): Google/SERP research, PAA/related searches, SEO tools such as Semrush when available, spreadsheets, SME input and their own judgement.

What the product does about each: Push \-\> compresses research, cleaning, clustering, prioritisation and website architecture into one run; Pull \-\> produces a prioritised page/content plan with evidence behind it; Anxiety \-\> grounds the initial structure and final recommendations in traceable real-search evidence, shows representative sourced queries at first value, and keeps final recommendations traceable to their underlying demand clusters and retained relevant queries/provenance; Habit \-\> preserves the marketer's judgement through Correct / Fix this and only asks about ambiguities that would materially change the output.  
The one worry onboarding must remove: "Is this real search demand, or is the AI just making up plausible-looking topics?"

# \#\# 3\. The core flow

Today (what they do now, before your product):  
1\. The offering is already established and a prioritised website page/content plan is needed.  
2\. Understand the offering, business context and domain using client/SME input and existing material.  
3\. Research demand through Google/SERPs, PAA/related searches and SEO tools such as Semrush.  
4\. Consolidate, export, clean and deduplicate the evidence in spreadsheets.  
5\. Group searches, interpret the underlying need/intent and classify the demand.  
6\. Prioritise which demand matters more or less.  
7\. Translate the demand into website coverage: pages, supporting content, sections and FAQs.  
8\. Package the recommendation, defend it, revise it if needed and hand off the approved plan into production.

With my product:  
1\. The offering is already established and the practitioner needs to decide what the website should cover.  
2\. They begin with the lowest-friction business context: a short description of one established offering on the Landing. After Start, Topic Gibbon progressively asks only the next useful context question, such as whether there is an existing website and whether there are up to 2 competitor/inspiration sites. V1 research geography is fixed to India, so the user is not asked to choose a target market.  
3\. The product performs a lightweight real-search retrieval and shows an initial grounded structure with 2–3 sourced query examples under each card.  
4\. They confirm the product's understanding using Correct / Fix this.  
5\. After first value, the full run is unlocked either by a one-time ₹399 UPI payment through Razorpay or by the single V1 invite code for 100% comped access. Without payment or a valid invite code, the run ends at first value. No account is required.  
6\. The product performs the heavy research/retrieval, cleaning, clustering by underlying need/intent, internal funnel classification and prioritisation. Each demand cluster retains the full relevant keyword/query set after cleaning, with provenance available for inspection; the practitioner reviews the synthesised demand map and answers only consequential ambiguities.  
7\. The product converts the prioritised demand into a final hierarchical website page/content plan. Each recommended node is labelled as Page / Section / FAQ / Supporting Content, shows relative priority and directional High / Medium / Low demand only when reliable external search-volume evidence is available; otherwise it shows Insufficient evidence, and remains traceable to one or more underlying demand clusters and their retained relevant search evidence/provenance.

Things it takes to get the job done today: 47–91+ meaningful actions · With my product: \~4 meaningful user actions

What must not happen:  
Step 3: The product must not invent demand or present AI-generated topics as real search evidence.  
Step 4: A wrong interpretation must not silently flow into the rest of the analysis; the user needs a simple way to correct it.  
Step 6: The product must not cluster by wording alone, hide genuine ambiguity, force the user to inspect the raw dataset, or infer High / Medium / Low demand from recurrence, breadth or AI intuition. High / Medium / Low must come from reliable external search-volume evidence such as DataForSEO; where reliable volume is unavailable, show Insufficient evidence.  
Step 7: The product must not prioritise by demand alone when business context materially changes the decision, must not turn every cluster into a website page, and must not hide the mapping between the final website recommendation and the underlying demand clusters/evidence.

Other flows (stop when the job is covered): Thin or contradictory context \-\> clarify only what materially affects the interpretation \-\> continue. Missing or unreliable search-volume evidence \-\> show Insufficient evidence, never Low \-\> continue. Material ambiguity \-\> ask the practitioner only if the answer would materially change clustering, prioritisation or architecture \-\> continue. Practitioner is also the decision maker \-\> skip any external approval workflow \-\> job done.
